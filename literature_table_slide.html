# Citation Core Contribution Key Finding Limitation
[1] Basecalling
Wick et al., 2019Genome Biology 20(1), 129 		Standard benchmark methodology for nanopore basecallers; evaluates individual-read and assembly-level consensus accuracy. Taxon-specific training substantially improves consensus accuracy, motivating training on bacterial isolates (NO-MISS). Benchmarks older RNN/HMM-era tools; may not fully reflect transformer-era accuracy.
[2] Basecalling
Xu et al. (Fast-Bonito), 2021AI in the Life Sciences 1, 100011 		Neural architecture search redesign of Bonito for GPU speed optimisation. 153.8% speed improvement on GPU with comparable/better accuracy; direct precedent for speed/accuracy trade-offs under compression. GPU-centric; no edge/embedded deployment analysis or power profiling.
[3] Basecalling
Pages-Gallego and De Ridder, 2023Genome Biology 24(1), 71 		Most rigorous standardised benchmark of basecaller architectures to date. Bonito CNN+LSTM+CRF design remains state-of-the-art; species bias in training data has large performance impact. Benchmark may lag behind rapid model updates (e.g., Dorado SUP v5 post-publication).
[4] Edge
Peresini et al. (DeepNano-coral), 2021Bioinformatics 37(24), 4661-4667 		Real-time edge basecalling on USB-attached Coral Edge TPU. Achieves real-time basecalling at only 10 W with accuracy comparable to Guppy fast mode; key power/throughput baseline for Jetson comparison. Single-task (basecalling only); no modification detection or transcript quantification head.
[5] Basecalling
Xie et al. (Coral), 2026Nature Comms 17(1), 1851 		Transformer encoder-decoder basecaller with dual context-awareness at signal and sequence levels for direct RNA sequencing. Up to 6.17% accuracy improvement over Dorado on direct RNA; useful architectural contrast to Dorado for our accuracy targets. RNA-focused; applicability to DNA basecalling and modification detection is not directly demonstrated.

Edge and Embedded Hardware (Section 6)
[6] Edge HW
Savari et al., 2025IEEE TVLSI 34(2), 606-619 		Dedicated FPGA-based RISC-V CTC Viterbi decoder accelerator for mobile DNA sequencers. Over 23,000 bases/second at only 0.43 W overhead power; hardware-accelerated decoding baseline for GPU inference comparison. FPGA-specific; not directly portable to Jetson TensorRT pipeline; sequence decoding only.
[7] Edge HW
Wu et al., 2025IEEE Trans. Comput. Biol. Bioinformatics 		Heterogeneous RISC-V SoC integrating basecalling and sequence-alignment accelerators. 11.5x and 1.2x energy efficiency over x86 CPUs and high-end GPUs at 83.7% basecalling accuracy; closest system-level competitor for Pareto-frontier analysis. No modification detection or multi-task head; custom SoC not commercially available for direct benchmarking.
Multi-Task Learning in Genomics (Section 3)
[8] Multi-Task
Luo et al. (Clairvoyante), 2019Nature Comms 10(1), 998 		Foundational multi-task CNN for genomics jointly predicting variant type, zygosity, allele, and indel length from a shared encoder. Directly analogous to our multi-head architecture design; establishes viability of shared-encoder multi-output genomic learning. Operates on pileup images, not raw nanopore signal; no modification detection component.
[9] Multi-Task
Xiong et al. (MASS), 2021Nucleic Acids Research 49(7), 3719-3734 		Multi-task curriculum learning applied to m6A RNA modification signal across multiple species simultaneously. Template for weighting and sequencing task losses during training; captures cross-species modification signals jointly. RNA modification focused; curriculum learning strategy may need adaptation for DNA nanopore basecalling context.
[10] Multi-Task
Wang et al., 2024Nature Comms 15(1), 7148 		Incremental learning plus anomaly detection to adapt basecalling models for modification-rich sequences. Close precedent for how Head A (sequence) and Head B (modification) interact within a shared backbone; shows adaptation feasibility. Two-stage sequential approach; not a single-pass joint multi-task model as proposed here.

Section 4 - Epigenetic Modification Detection - References [11-14]

# Citation Core Contribution Key Finding Limitation
[11] Modification
Liu et al. (DeepMod), 2019Nature Comms 10(1), 2449 		Foundational bidirectional RNN for 5mC/6mA detection from raw Oxford Nanopore signal. High precision (0.9+) for 5mC/6mA; establishes baseline accuracy our multi-task Head B must approach under a shared compressed encoder. Requires dedicated modification-calling model; no joint basecalling; based on older FAST5 format, not POD5.
[12] Modification
Ni et al. (DeepSignal), 2019Bioinformatics 35(22), 4586-4595 		Deep-learning DNA methylation detection from Nanopore reads using bidirectional RNN on FAST5. High precision (0.9+) for methylation calling; companion foundational baseline alongside DeepMod for Head B accuracy bar. RNN-only; single-task; not adapted for modern R10.4.1 chemistry or POD5 file format.
[13] Modification
Ahsan et al. (DeepMod2), 2024Nature Comms 15(1), 1448 		Most direct modern comparator: BiLSTM and Transformer variants for modification calling on POD5/FAST5 files. Performance comparable to closed-source Guppy/Dorado; essential accuracy benchmark for our POD5-based pipeline. Single-task modification model only; does not jointly perform basecalling or transcript classification.
[14] Modification
Kulkarni et al., 2026Nature Comms (in press) 		Most recent systematic cross-species benchmark of nanopore methylation tools covering 5mC (non-CpG), 6mA, and 4mC detection. Newer models substantially improve non-CpG 5mC, 6mA, and 4mC detection; directly informs our modification evaluation protocol structure. Benchmarking study only; no novel architecture; may miss very latest models at time of our submission.

Model Compression and Quantization (Section 5)
[15] Compression
Salah et al., 2025Integration, 102508 		Post-training INT8 quantization (PTQ) of FP32 CNNs (VGG16, ResNet50) via asymmetric quantization for FPGA deployment without retraining. Up to 67% throughput gain, 68% latency reduction, and substantial Power-Delay Product gains at marginal accuracy loss; frames our less than 5% degradation target as realistic. Applied to vision CNNs on FPGA, not nanopore signal models or TensorRT/Jetson pipeline; genomics-specific PTQ literature is essentially absent.
Dataset Provenance - Two-Dataset Design (Section 7)
[16] Dataset
Galeone et al., 2025BMC Genomics 26(1), 394 		Profiles methylation patterns across four public-health-relevant bacterial species including Enterococcus faecium using Dorado SUP v5 on R10.4.1 chemistry. Primary literature anchor justifying SRR32250932 (PRJNA1091452) as a valid, well-characterised independent test target with documented 5mC/6mA motifs. Species-limited (four species); may not represent broader genomic diversity required for generalisation claims.
[17] Dataset
Dabernig-Heinz et al., 2025Scientific Data 12(1), 1905 		Companion dataset paper from same group: 79-isolate multicenter nanopore raw-signal resource supporting re-basecalling and nucleotide-modification reanalysis. Methodological context for our .pod5 conversion and re-basecalling pipeline; validates train/test independence of PRJNA1091452. Data descriptor only; no novel algorithmic contribution; raw signal quality varies across isolates and sequencing centres.

Literature Gaps and Novelty

Summary of gaps addressed by this work - Section 8

Mature Sub-fields (Strong Precedent)

  • Basecalling architectures [1-5] - well benchmarked
  • Multi-task genomic modelling [8-10] - established
  • Modification detection [11-14] - mature, high precision

Partial Coverage

  • Edge basecalling [4, 6, 7] - sequence decoding only; no joint modification detection or transcript prediction
  • PTQ for CNNs [15] - vision architectures only; not applied to nanopore signal models

Unaddressed Intersection - Our Novelty

  • No prior work jointly addresses: (a) single-pass multi-task nanopore architecture + (b) INT8 PTQ via TensorRT + (c) real edge deployment with full power/throughput/accuracy benchmarking
  • No prior nanopore basecalling or modification-detection model has been INT8-quantized via TensorRT for edge deployment
  • This framework is a genuine methodological synthesis, not an incremental extension of any single prior work
